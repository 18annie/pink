Understanding Breast Cancer

Breast cancer awareness is more than just a pink ribbon — it's about education, support, and proactive health management. Understanding the facts and your body is the first step toward prevention and early intervention.

0 in 8

Women may be affected during their lifetime. Global statistics highlight the importance of staying informed and vigilant.

Early Action

Knowing changes in your body can help you seek appropriate medical advice immediately, significantly improving outcomes.

Professional Care

Screening and medical evaluation are important parts of breast health. Regular check-ups with professionals are essential.

Know the Signs. Know Your Body.

Recognizing what is normal for you is the best way to notice any unusual changes. If you experience any of these, consult a healthcare provider.

New lump or mass

A painless, hard mass that has irregular edges is more likely to be cancer, but some can be tender and soft.

Breast swelling

Swelling of all or part of a breast, even if no lump is felt, should be evaluated by a medical professional.

Skin changes

Skin irritation, dimpling, redness, scaliness, or thickening of the nipple or breast skin area.

Nipple changes

Nipple retraction (turning inward) or pain in the nipple area are signs that require attention.

Unusual discharge

Nipple discharge other than breast milk (especially if it is bloody) is an important sign to check.

Change in size or shape

Any significant change in the size or the shape of the breast that is unusual for you.

Self-awareness is the foundation of breast health. Being familiar with how your breasts look and feel makes it easier to spot changes. We encourage everyone to make breast health a part of their overall wellness routine.
